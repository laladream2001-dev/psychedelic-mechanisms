# How Classical Psychedelics Reshape Perception: A Clear Guide to 5-HT2A and the REBUS Model

*By Nguyen Van Lang*

Classic psychedelics such as psilocybin, LSD, DMT, and mescaline are known for producing vivid changes in perception, emotion, and self-awareness. Modern neuroscience explains these effects neutrally by looking at how they interact with specific brain receptors and large-scale networks.




1. The 5-HT2A Receptor: The Primary Entry Point

Research shows that classical psychedelics work mainly by activating the serotonin 2A receptor (5-HT2AR), which is densely located on layer 5 pyramidal neurons in the cerebral cortex—especially in areas handling vision and high-level thinking (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6588209/; Banushi & Polito, 2023: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10669348/). Blocking this receptor with ketanserin prevents most acute psychedelic effects.




2. Opening the Sensory Gate (CSTC Model)

Normally, the thalamus acts like a filter, deciding how much sensory information reaches conscious awareness. Under LSD, brain imaging shows that this filter relaxes, allowing more sensory and internal signals to flow between the thalamus and the cortex (Preller et al., 2019: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6377471/; Kwan et al., 2022: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9641582/).




3. Relaxing Rigid Brain Habits (The REBUS Model)

Brain scans also show reduced synchronization within the Default Mode Network (DMN)—a system linked to self-reflection—alongside greater communication across the whole brain (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6588209/). The REBUS (RElaxed Beliefs Under pSychedelics) model proposes that psychedelics temporarily soften the brain's strongly held predictions, allowing sensory data and fresh perspectives to surface.
