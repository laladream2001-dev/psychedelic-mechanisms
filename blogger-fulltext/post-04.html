CHAPTER 4: DISSOCIATIVE HALLUCINOGENS — KETAMINE AND NMDA RECEPTOR ANTAGONISM

Molecular Mechanisms & Dual Hypotheses

Ketamine operates primarily as a use-dependent, uncompetitive open-channel antagonist of the ionotropic N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor (NMDAR), binding to the phencyclidine (PCP) site within the open channel pore (MacDonald et al., 1991; Marguilho et al., 2023). In addition, recent structural evidence demonstrates that ketamine and PCP can directly bind and activate human opioid receptors (including MOR and KOR), although the extent to which opioid receptor engagement contributes to ketamine’s distinct clinical effects remains an emerging finding requiring further evaluation (Jiang et al., 2026). Two main cellular hypotheses—investigated primarily in preclinical models—have been proposed mainly to account for ketamine’s rapid antidepressant and neuroplasticity-related effects rather than its acute dissociative phenomenology (Marguilho et al., 2023). Specifically, the eEF2–BDNF direct-inhibition pathway has not been demonstrated to explain acute dissociation; instead, Marguilho et al. (2023) separately propose network-level explanations for ketamine’s acute dissociative and psychedelic-like phenomena (discussed below): Schematic Summary (Preclinical Ketamine Cellular Hypotheses for Antidepressant/Plasticity-Related Effects): Two principal preclinical pathways proposed for rapid antidepressant and plasticity-related effects converge on BDNF signaling and mTORC1-associated synaptogenesis, without explaining acute dissociation (Marguilho et al., 2023): (1) the Disinhibition Hypothesis—blockade of NMDARs on GABAergic (SST/PV) interneurons reduces cortical inhibition, triggering a glutamate surge, postsynaptic AMPAR activation, Ca2+ influx via VGCCs, and activity-dependent BDNF release; and (2) the Direct Inhibition Hypothesis—blockade of tonic resting-state NMDARs on pyramidal neurons suppresses eEF2K, promoting eEF2 dephosphorylation and rapid dendritic BDNF translation. Disinhibition Hypothesis: Low sub-anesthetic doses of ketamine selectively block NMDARs present on tonically active γ-aminobutyric acid (GABAergic) interneurons (e.g., somatostatin- and parvalbumin-expressing interneurons) (Marguilho et al., 2023). This disinhibits cortical excitatory pyramidal neurons, causing a transient surge in extracellular glutamate. The glutamate surge activates postsynaptic α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid receptors (AMPARs), driving depolarization, entry of Ca2+ through voltage-gated channels, activity-dependent release of BDNF, and activation of the mTOR Complex 1 (mTORC1) cascade (Marguilho et al., 2023). Direct Inhibition Hypothesis: Direct blockade of resting, spontaneously active postsynaptic NMDARs on pyramidal neurons suppresses eukaryotic elongation factor 2 kinase (eEF2K) activity. Dephosphorylation of eEF2 relieves translation suppression of BDNF, leading to rapid BDNF protein synthesis and downstream dendritic spine consolidation (Marguilho et al., 2023). ### Reward Circuitry and Habenular Bursting In preclinical rodent models, ketamine targets the lateral habenula (LHb), a subcortical region that acts as an “anti-reward center” by suppressing dopamine release from the ventral tegmental area (VTA) during negative reward prediction errors (Marguilho et al., 2023). Depressive states are characterized by pathological, hyperactive NMDA- and T-type voltage-gated Ca2+ channel-dependent burst firing in LHb neurons (Marguilho et al., 2023). Ketamine rapidly blocks NMDAR-dependent bursting in the LHb. This can disinhibit downstream monoaminergic reward centers and has been proposed to contribute to antidepressant effects (Marguilho et al., 2023). ### Unified Theoretical/Conceptual Model (Salience vs. Default Mode Network) Unlike 5-HT2A receptors, which concentrate heavily in high-order transmodal association and visual networks, NMDARs exhibit broad (though non-uniform) expression across the cerebral cortex rather than restriction to transmodal hubs. Within this broader cortical distribution compared with 5-HT2ARs, Marguilho et al. (2023) propose a distinct conceptual network impact: Schematic Summary — Conceptual Model-Based Dose-Dependent Neural Dynamics of Ketamine (Qualitative Conceptual Levels): In Marguilho et al.’s (2023) conceptual framework, ketamine’s neural effects are organized across three qualitative exposure levels—lower sub-anesthetic, moderate sub-anesthetic, and anesthetic—without fixed numerical cutoffs, because actual pharmacodynamic thresholds depend critically on species, route, formulation (e.g., racemic vs. enantiomeric), and rate of administration. Within this conceptual framework: (1) the Lower Sub-anesthetic Level may disrupt the Salience Network (SN) and relax high-level bodily/minimal-self priors (distinct from low-level sensory priors); (2) the Moderate Sub-anesthetic Level may expand disruption to the Default Mode Network (DMN), relaxing highest-level narrative/autobiographical priors and potentially inducing ego dissolution; and (3) the Anesthetic Level may induce alternating slow-delta and high-frequency gamma bursts alongside anesthetic unresponsiveness. Lower Sub-anesthetic Level (conceptual level in Marguilho et al., 2023): Because effective exposures depend on species, route, formulation, and rate of administration, this level is framed qualitatively rather than as a fixed quantitative threshold (Marguilho et al., 2023). In Marguilho et al.’s (2023) conceptual model, ketamine at lower sub-anesthetic exposures may preferentially disrupt the Salience Network (SN; insular and anterior cingulate cortices)—which in their framework encodes high-level priors concerning the body and minimal self (positioned below the narrative self in the cortical hierarchy, rather than representing low-level sensory priors)—relaxing these bodily/minimal-self priors and potentially inducing dissociative depersonalization and derealization (Marguilho et al., 2023). Moderate Sub-anesthetic Level (conceptual level in Marguilho et al., 2023): Likewise dependent on species, route, formulation, and administration rate rather than defined by a universal numerical cutoff, disruption at moderate sub-anesthetic exposures in this conceptual classification may expand into the Default Mode Network (DMN), relaxing the highest-level narrative and autobiographical priors and potentially inducing psychedelic-like ego dissolution (Marguilho et al., 2023). Anesthetic Level (conceptual level in Marguilho et al., 2023): Contingent on species, route, formulation, and administration rate rather than a single standardized dose figure, ketamine at anesthetic levels may induce alternating slow-delta oscillations and high-frequency gamma bursts (the “ketamine rhythm”), suppressing large-scale cortical communication and producing an anesthetic state of unresponsiveness (Marguilho et al., 2023).
