CHAPTER 3: SYSTEMS NEUROSCIENCE OF CLASSICAL PSYCHEDELICS — CSTC, CCC, DMN, AND REBUS

Cortico-Striato-Thalamo-Cortical (CSTC) Loop Gating

The Cortico-Striato-Thalamo-Cortical (CSTC) model is a theoretical/systems-level framework positing that the thalamus serves as an activity-dependent filter regulating feedforward sensory and interoceptive information flow to the cerebral cortex (Preller et al., 2019; Kwan et al., 2022). Schematic Summary (CSTC Loop Gating): In the theoretical CSTC model, cortical layer 5 pyramidal neurons project through basal ganglia inhibitory output pathways (via striatum and pallidal output nuclei such as GPi) to regulate gating over the relay thalamus, modulating feedforward sensory flow to the cortex (Doss et al., 2022; Kwan et al., 2022). Empirically in humans under LSD (n = 25), Dynamic Causal Modeling (DCM) effective connectivity from the thalamus to the PCC increases in a ketanserin-sensitive, 5-HT2A-dependent manner, whereas positive/excitatory ventral striatum-to-thalamus DCM effective connectivity decreases independently of ketanserin blockade (Preller et al., 2019). Within this theoretical model, 5-HT2AR stimulation on cortical L5 and striatal neurons has been hypothesized to alter basal ganglia inhibitory output pathways to the thalamus, loosening the “thalamic filter” (Doss et al., 2022; Kwan et al., 2022). Importantly, this anatomical basal ganglia inhibitory output pathway must be distinguished from Dynamic Causal Modeling (DCM) effective connectivity estimated between selected regional BOLD signals: in Preller et al.’s (2019) DCM model across 25 human participants, ventral striatum-to-thalamus effective connectivity carries a positive (excitatory) sign, so a decrease in that model parameter is not itself a measurement of a direct inhibitory striato-thalamic projection or of reduced GABA release. Empirically, Preller et al. (2019) demonstrated a pharmacological dissociation between the two modeled CSTC branches under LSD: increased thalamus-to-posterior cingulate cortex (PCC) effective connectivity was ketanserin-sensitive (dependent on 5-HT2A receptor activation), whereas decreased ventral striatum-to-thalamus effective connectivity was ketanserin-insensitive (independent of 5-HT2A receptor blockade) (Preller et al., 2019; Kwan et al., 2022). Separately from Preller et al. (2019), deficits in Prepulse Inhibition (PPI) of the acoustic startle reflex have been discussed in the broader CSTC literature as a behavioral index of altered sensorimotor gating, though PPI was not assessed in Preller et al.’s effective connectivity study. ### Claustro-Cortical Circuit (CCC) Model The claustrum—a thin, highly interconnected subcortical nucleus—expresses abundant 5-HT2A receptors, forming the basis of the cortico-claustro-cortical (CCC) model of psychedelic action (Barrett et al., 2020; Doss et al., 2022). In human functional neuroimaging, psilocybin altered functional connectivity of the right claustrum bidirectionally: it decreased functional connectivity between the right claustrum and both the auditory network and the Default Mode Network (DMN), while increasing functional connectivity between the right claustrum and the frontoparietal task control network (Barrett et al., 2020). Regarding subjective effects, Barrett et al. (2020) reported exploratory associations involving claustrum BOLD signal amplitude/variance and ratings such as overall drug intensity, mystical experience, and ineffability, rather than showing that these three specific right-claustrum connectivity changes correlated with ego dissolution or cognitive reorganization; moreover, those exploratory subjective associations did not remain statistically significant after multiple-comparison correction (Barrett et al., 2020). ### Default Mode Network, Alpha Oscillations, and the Entropic Brain Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) and magnetoencephalography (MEG) demonstrate that classical psychedelics disrupt established large-scale brain networks (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019): DMN Disintegration: The Default Mode Network (comprising the PCC, medial prefrontal cortex [mPFC], and angular gyrus) exhibits marked within-network functional desynchronization under psilocybin and LSD (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019). Global Integration: Concurrently, localized functional network boundaries undergo functional desegregation, yielding an increase in cross-network functional connectivity (e.g., functional coupling between visual cortex and auditory and somatosensory networks, without implying new anatomical connections) (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019). Oscillatory Suppression: MEG studies reveal dramatic, broadband reductions in spontaneous oscillatory power, most prominently in the alpha frequency band within the PCC (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019). The magnitude of alpha power suppression within the PCC is associated with subjective ratings of ego dissolution (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019). Entropic Brain Hypothesis: In this theoretical model, psychedelics elevate Lempel-Ziv signal complexity in cortical electrophysiological recordings, shifting macro-scale neural dynamics away from rigid, constrained regimes toward a state characterized by increased informational entropy and stronger signatures of criticality (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019). ### REBUS and the Anarchic Brain The REBUS (RElaxed Beliefs Under pSychedelics) model is a theoretical framework that integrates the Entropic Brain Hypothesis with hierarchical Bayesian predictive processing and the Free-Energy Principle (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019). Schematic Summary (REBUS Hierarchical Predictive Processing): Within this theoretical framework, high-level priors (DMN/mPFC, with deep-layer L5 pyramidal neurons hypothesized to encode posterior expectations/priors) send top-down predictions downward across cortical layers, while unconstrained afferent sensory and interoceptive streams send bottom-up prediction errors surging upward when high-level prior weighting is relaxed via 5-HT2AR agonism (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019). In the theoretical framework of hierarchical predictive coding, the brain generates top-down predictions (priors) that constrain and interpret bottom-up sensory information (prediction errors). Deep-layer cortical L5 pyramidal neurons are hypothesized to encode posterior expectations (priors), rather than the precision weighting of those priors (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019). Within this theoretical model, by directly activating 5-HT2AR on L5 neurons, classical psychedelics desynchronize deep-layer activity, selectively decreasing the precision weighting of high-level priors (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019). This process “flattens” the free-energy landscape (analogous to simulated annealing in optimization algorithms). As high-level cognitive constraints relax: Unconstrained bottom-up sensory and emotional prediction errors cascade up the hierarchy into conscious awareness, causing visual distortions and unprompted memory surfacing (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019). Rigid, pathological high-level priors (e.g., negative self-schemas in major depressive disorder or compulsive loops in OCD) become malleable, theoretically allowing Bayesian Model Reduction (BMR) to reorganize belief architectures upon acute drug clearance (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019).
